Your 2020 emergency spending is still on the books. The useful question is which of it earned its place.

We went back through what held up at Denver-area small businesses three years on. Four things did: managing by deliverables instead of hours, one collaboration stack configured once, a wider hiring radius for hard-to-fill back-office roles, and processes written down so "just ask Susan" stopped being a policy.

What turned out to be a band-aid: the pile of free-trial apps from 2020, one policy for every role, and the assumed savings. Office rent came down, but tooling, hardware refresh and the management time hybrid coordination eats took a real bite out of the gain.

The part most small firms skip is remote access. When people went home, company data went onto home networks and personal devices. Multi-factor authentication on email, accounting and every remote login is the floor, and most cyber-insurance renewals now ask for it. Managed devices and a restore you have actually tested come next.

One takeaway you can use this quarter: sort every role into on-site, hybrid or remote-eligible, write the rule per bucket instead of per person, and pick anchor days. Then check when your lease renews, because that date, not the loudest department, sets your timeline.

The full piece walks through the policy steps, the security baseline and the watch-outs. Worth a read before your next renewal.
