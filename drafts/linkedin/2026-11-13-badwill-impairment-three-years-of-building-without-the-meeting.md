Three years ago I was not invited to the meeting about the cash flow imperative. So I kept a ledger of what got built instead.

It lists six systems, each with a link and an auditor's note. A control plane over 44 repositories. A content system where AI agents are users whose work arrives as a diff. A legal research tool that can keep every prompt on the local machine. A CSV editor that is finished and archived. Where an entry is still a plan, it is booked as a plan.

The argument underneath is about where controls live. A large company manages risk with meetings: people remember the rules and agree again that they apply. These projects manage it with files and checks. The rules live in the repository, machines verify every change, agents draft and open pull requests, and a named person approves anything that leaves the building.

That is not a talent story. It is the same approach we use with small businesses, and you can borrow it. Pick the recurring meeting whose real job is catching a mistake, write that mistake down as a check a script can run, and keep the meeting until the check has caught something twice.

The essay has the full ledger, screenshots included, and four more things to borrow. Read it if you suspect your meetings are doing a machine's job.
